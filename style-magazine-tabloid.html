Local startup's machine remembers everything you've ever told it — and it plays to win

STORYTELLER WIRE SERVICE — MARCH 23 — URGENT

BREAKING A new artificial intelligence system developed by kåjøda can now hold the thread of your entire story — across sessions, across sessions, across time itself.

The system, known internally as the Narrator, does not summarize. It does not forget. Every name you gave a character, every door you chose not to open, every alliance you broke — it is all there. Every time.

Rivals in the space have described the development as "alarming." Industry observers used the word "unfair." The Storyteller team used one word: "obvious."

"Of course it should remember everything," said a source close to the project who declined to be named because they were standing right next to the terminal. "That's the whole point."

EXCLUSIVE The DJ, the Pathfinder, and the Unnamed Systems

Two sub-systems of the Play Engine — the DJ and the Pathfinder — have been confirmed by sources with direct access to the specification documents. The DJ governs story pacing, tone, and the emotional arc of a session. The Pathfinder manages narrative branching logic.

Neither is built. Both are, by all accounts, described in enough detail that building them is "merely an engineering problem." The distinction between specification and implementation continues to be treated as significant at kåjøda.

THE GUIDE SPEAKS:
HOW CORRECTIONS WORK WITHOUT BREAKING THE SPELL

STORYTELLER WIRE SERVICE — MARCH 23 — SPECIAL REPORT

The most dangerous moment in any interactive story is not the dragon. It is the moment the system gets something wrong and a human has to fix it.

Every competitor in the AI storytelling space handles this moment the same way: they stop the story. A dialog box appears. A correction is made. The story, stunned and embarrassed, resumes. The spell is broken. Players leave.

kåjøda has named this problem and built a system around it. The Guide is a behind-the-scenes conversational layer that intercepts player corrections before they reach the Narrator. It absorbs the correction. It updates the record. It feeds the correction back into the story without the story ever knowing it happened.

Storytime is never broken to do it. This is the rule. It is not aspirational. It is architectural.

DIAGRAM: GUIDE FLOW

ABOVE: The Guide intercept architecture — player input enters left, correction is absorbed by Guide layer (center), adjusted context returns to Narrator (right) without session interruption

GRAPHIC: STORYTELLER VISUAL DESK / FILE DIAGRAM

The Guide can handle: name corrections ("I said his name was Marcus, not Marco"), setting corrections ("This is a spaceship, not a castle"), continuity corrections ("She was already dead by then"), and what one spec document calls "vibe corrections" — adjustments to the emotional tone of the story without stopping to explain why.

Vibe corrections are, sources say, the hardest. They are also the most important. The system is designed to sense them before they are explicitly stated. Whether it does this reliably in production is, as of press time, a matter of ongoing specification.

The design principle is simple, and it is total: the player should never feel like they are correcting a computer. They should feel like they are telling a very attentive collaborator something new about the world. The world updates. The story continues. Nobody notices the seam.

STORY PACKAGES: WHAT AUTHORS WILL BUILD

The Create Engine will allow authors to build Story Packages — self-contained narrative modules in TOME format. Each package contains characters, arcs, worlds, and the rules of the story's physics.

Authors set the stage. The Narrator runs the show. The player never sees the seam between authored intent and live improvisation.

FINAL THOUGHTS

A STORY
IS NOT
A PRODUCT

Every company that has tried to build AI-powered interactive fiction has made the same mistake. They have built a product that tells stories. They have not built something that cares whether the story lands.

The Narrator cares. The architecture cares. The six-section vault, the cross-cutting concept policy, the Guide intercept layer, the TOME format, the player archetype model — all of it is infrastructure built in service of a single moment: the moment a player feels, in their gut, that the world they are in is real.

That moment cannot be optimized. It cannot be A/B tested. It can only be designed for, with patience, with specificity, and with a level of documentation density that should alarm any reasonable investor.

kåjøda is not reasonable. That is, the Daily has concluded, the point.
