01 / THE SIGNAL

The Time Traveller

"You must follow me carefully. I shall have to controvert one or two ideas that are almost universally accepted." — H.G. Wells, The Time Machine (1895)

The Time Traveller — for so it will be convenient to speak of him — was expounding a recondite matter to us. His grey eyes shone and twinkled, and his usually pale face was flushed and animated. The fire burned brightly, and the soft radiance of the incandescent lights in the lilies of silver caught the bubbles that flashed and passed in our glasses.

"There are really four dimensions, three which we call the three planes of Space, and a fourth, Time. There is, however, a tendency to draw an unreal distinction between the former three dimensions and the latter, because it happens that our consciousness moves intermittently in one direction along the latter from the beginning to the end of our lives."

He was in an experimental mood. He had been reading very carefully into the deeper possibilities of space and time — possibilities that he now intended, with full confidence, to demonstrate. The incandescent lights played across the glass models on the mantelpiece; somewhere outside, the night was closing in fast.

// SYSTEM NOTE   The ability to tell a story across four dimensions — space, time, consequence, and feeling — is the premise. The Narrator holds all four.

02 / THE MACHINE

The Demonstration

"Can a cube that does not last for any time at all, have a real existence?" He was pacing rapidly about the room, and turned and faced us. "Clearly," the Time Traveller proceeded, "any real body must have extension in four directions: it must have Length, Breadth, Thickness, and — Duration."

"But wait — I have not shown you the model yet. Watch carefully." — H.G. Wells, The Time Machine (1895)

He took something out of his breast-pocket — a glittering metallic framework, scarcely larger than a small clock, and very delicately made. There was ivory in it, and some transparent crystalline substance. And now I must be explicit, for this that follows — unless his explanation is to be accepted — is an absolutely unaccountable thing.

He handed it to Filby, who handled it admiringly. "Those are ivory pieces." The Time Traveller laughed. The psychologist did the same. The other looked more doubtful than he had been before, but not more unbelieving. The Time Traveller held his hand out and rested it on the edge of the table.
