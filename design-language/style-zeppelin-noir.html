The Design Language

What the aesthetic is doing

Soot black and aged brass. The weight of aluminum and ambition. This palette belongs to a particular kind of darkness — not the flat black of a void, but the layered dark of a mahogany smoking lounge at three in the morning, when the lamps have been turned low and everyone who is still awake has a reason to be.

Zeppelin Noir is a design language for stories where the world has not yet decided what it is. Alternate histories. Contested timelines. The kind of story where the murderer might be the least surprising thing in the room.

"The airship does not ask where you are going. Only where you have been."

The Ship

Six hundred feet of aluminum and ambition. Forty-two passengers. Twelve crew. One murderer.

The Storm

Static electricity on the hull. Navigation unreliable. No radio contact since 0100.

The Clock

Landfall in 31 hours. The killer knows this too.

Incident Report — 0300 Hrs

The body was found in the smoking lounge.

The smooth dark head turned. The eyes that had been on Spade's face looked away now — looked at the table before them, at the backs of their own hands, at the amber circle of lamplight on the mahogany. Below the gondola windows the Atlantic moved in darkness. The airship hummed its particular hum, that sound like the inside of a kept promise.

He had been a careful man in life, whoever he was. His papers were too good, which was to say, too complete — the kind of completeness that requires effort, which is not the same as authenticity. He sat in the leather chair near the forward observation window and he had not moved in a way that suggested sleep. The steward knew the difference.

"The airship does not ask where you are going. Only where you have been."

The smoking lounge was the kind of room that kept confidences. Mahogany panels. Ashtrays that had been emptied and refilled many times over. The smell of tobacco and altitude and something underneath that — an older smell, which is the smell of secrets that have been kept too long in small spaces.

Outside, the Atlantic was nothing. Below the gondola windows: darkness, then more darkness, then wherever the ocean ended and the sky began, which was a distinction the night had decided not to make.

Passenger Dossiers

Everyone had a reason to be on board.

The manifest lists forty-two names. Forty-one of them had destinations. The forty-second was going somewhere that destinations don't cover.

Eleanor Voss, 44

Last seen near the telegraph room at midnight. Traveling alone. No stated purpose. Her luggage contains three identical dresses and a collection of maps for cities she has not listed as destinations.

Raymond Ashby, 31

Military posture. Civilian papers. The fingers of a man who has held a gun. He ordered bourbon at 2200, again at 0030, and was last confirmed in his berth at 0115. His occupation is listed as "consultant."

Dr. Constance Mira, 58

Ship's physician. Arrived with two cases of medical equipment and one locked trunk. The trunk has not been opened. She volunteered to examine the body before anyone asked her to. She was not surprised by what she found.

Flight Log Recovered

What the storm looked like from inside

The airship rode the weather the way certain people ride misfortune — not gracefully, exactly, but with long practice. The captain had seen worse. He had seen storms that turned the compass into decoration. He had seen passengers who were not what their papers said. He had, once before, seen a man in a leather chair who was not sleeping. You did not forget that. What you did instead was continue to fly the ship, which was the only thing left to do.

The lightning, when it came, illuminated the hull from outside — the whole great silver body of the ship lit briefly silver-white, every rivet visible, every seam. From inside the observation windows it looked like the ship had a conscience and was briefly, painfully, aware of it.

There is a kind of silence that follows an announcement that something has gone wrong. It is not the absence of sound. It is the presence of everyone listening to what they will say next, and deciding not to say it yet.

"Someone on this ship knows exactly what happened. The question is whether they'll still be alive when we land."
