L. Frank Baum — The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, 1900

[ Illustration ]

Plate I — Kansas Cyclone, watercolor

The cyclone had set the house down very gently — for a cyclone — in the midst of a country of marvelous beauty.

There were lovely patches of greensward all about, with stately trees bearing rich and luscious fruits. Banks of gorgeous flowers were on every hand, and birds with rare and brilliant plumage sang and fluttered in the trees and bushes. A little way off was a small brook, rushing and sparkling along between green banks, and murmuring in a voice very grateful to a little girl who had lived so long on the dry, gray prairies.

While she stood looking eagerly at the strange and beautiful sights, she noticed coming toward her a group of the queerest people she had ever seen. They were not as big as the grown folk she had always been used to; but neither were they very small. In fact, they seemed about as tall as Dorothy, who was a well-grown child for her age, although they were, so far as looks go, many years older.

Three were men and one a woman, and all were oddly dressed. They wore round hats that rose to a small point a foot above their heads, with little bells around the brims that tinkled sweetly as they moved.

"You are welcome, most noble Sorceress, to the land of the Munchkins. We are so grateful to you for having killed the Wicked Witch of the East, and for setting our people free from bondage."
— The Good Witch of the North, Chapter II

The little woman walked up to Dorothy, made a low bow and said, in a sweet voice: "You are welcome, most noble Sorceress, to the land of the Munchkins. We are so grateful to you for having killed the Wicked Witch of the East, and for setting our people free from bondage."

Dorothy listened to this speech with wonder. What could the little woman mean by calling her a sorceress, and saying she had killed the Wicked Witch of the East? Dorothy was an innocent, harmless little girl, who had been carried by a cyclone many miles from home; and she had never killed anything in all her life.

Editor's note

On Arrival Stories

Every great interactive story is an arrival story. The player lands somewhere unfamiliar. The world is already in motion. The question — always — is what do you do next?

Baum understood this. Storyteller does too. The house lands. The witch is dead. The adventure begins before the character takes a single step.

Feature p.03
Deep Dive

What Storyteller Is

Not a chatbot. Not a game master simulator. Not another wrapper around a language model that pretends it has a soul.

Storyteller is a voice-first AI platform for interactive fiction — the kind that feels alive. The kind where your choices matter and the world responds like it was always waiting for you to arrive.

The difference is in what we refuse to do. We refuse to break the scene to manage the rules. We refuse to let the machine's uncertainty become the player's problem. We refuse to let the interface get between you and the story.

Magic by default. The Guide as the human interface for corrections. Storytime is never broken to do it.

Misregistration

The signature effect of Risograph printing: two ink drums slightly out of alignment. The coral ghost bleeds 3px right and 2px up from the teal master. On hover, it jitters.

In Riso, this is an accident. Here, it's the whole point. Imperfection as brand voice. The machine shows its seams and calls it beautiful.

Ink Pass 2

The Overlap Zone

Where teal and coral print on top of each other, the inks mix to create a muddy olive-brown — #7a5c28. Not a mistake. A physical truth.

No digital color picker would land here deliberately. But the press does it every time, and it's exactly right.
